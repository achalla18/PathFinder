PathFinder

Walking routes that know about curbs, slopes and sidewalks.

For people who use a wheelchair, a walker, a cane or a stroller, the fastest route is often not one they can take. PathFinder plans around missing curb ramps, steep grades and streets without sidewalks, and says plainly what nobody has recorded yet.

The data gap in Maryland's 6th district

Washington publishes its sidewalks, curb ramps and signals as open data, so PathFinder can plan with confidence there. Gaithersburg and Frederick have no open inventory yet: the bars show how much of each town is still unrecorded.

That gap can be closed by the community. A mapping campaign in OpenStreetMap, starting in Frederick, records sidewalks street by street, and every tag reaches PathFinder with the next data refresh.

Honest about what it knows

Every curb, slope and sidewalk carries its source and date. Where nothing is recorded, PathFinder says so instead of guessing, and you choose how much uncertainty you accept.
